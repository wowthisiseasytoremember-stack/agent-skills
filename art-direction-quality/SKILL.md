# Art Direction & Visual Quality — Agent Operating Standard

## Core rule

**Do not ask an agent to invent the aesthetic and then mark its own first draft as finished.** Assign an approved visual target, preserve editorial intent, implement it across every screen and state, capture the rendered result, inspect it, and correct visible defects.

This skill applies to user-facing web/mobile interfaces and interactive editorial packages. It is a quality overlay, **not** a mandatory aesthetic, a generic design template, or a demand to clone a third-party site.

## Inputs / precedence

1. Existing project-specific, user-approved design system, assets, copy, and creative direction (highest priority).
2. Current product/user requirements, flow, accessibility, and performance constraints.
3. Selected reference screenshots/sites and approved concept.
4. This cross-project quality overlay (fallback process and verification).

Do not re-open locked creative decisions, invent a new angle, replace an established brand, or impose a house style. When references conflict, record the unresolved choice and preserve the canonical design. Honor existing work claims/locks; use an isolated branch and leave evidence for reviewers.

## Operating sequence

### 1. Inspect before changing

- Identify what is live, approved, in progress, or experimental. Read DESIGN.md, AGENTS.md, style tokens, existing screens and PRs where present.
- Identify the target audience, the *single primary outcome*, and the task's importance (conversion, utility, expressive portfolio, editorial recognition).
- Classify project mode:
  - **Expressive/showcase**: distinctive metaphor, art and motion can lead.
  - **Utility/productivity**: task completion, information architecture, and clarity lead.
  - **Editorial/media**: recognition, framing consistency, readability, and repeatability lead.
- Preserve successful current elements; redesign only scoped failures.

### 2. Establish a reference-backed visual contract

Before implementation, record these fields in a short design brief or the project's existing DESIGN.md:

- Outcome and audience; primary flow and success criterion.
- **North-star visual reference** (screenshot or site URL); up to two supporting references with specific *extractable* traits. Credit sources, check licenses, do not copy logos/assets/text without rights.
- One-sentence art direction and what must feel different from a template.
- Typography: face(s), scale, hierarchy, line length.
- Layout: grid, density, whitespace, surface and component geometry.
- Color: purposeful palette and semantic states, including dark mode if supported.
- Imagery/art: authored image, icon, texture, 3D, photo or video treatment; provenance.
- Interaction and motion: why they exist, input methods, reduced-motion alternatives.
- Copy voice: nouns, tone, sentence length, claims, CTA style and disallowed phrasing.
- Anti-goals, constraints, approved exceptions, and accessible fallback.

Do not accept “clean”, “premium”, “modern”, “beautiful”, “bold” or “less AI” alone as an actionable specification. Convert adjectives into visible rules with examples.

### 3. Cover the whole experience, not the hero

Create a surface/state matrix appropriate to scope:

| Surface | Required states |
| --- | --- |
| Landing / first-run | First visit, mobile, navigation, primary CTA |
| Main workflow | Default, selection, edit, save, success |
| Secondary routes | List, detail, settings/preferences, help |
| System states | Empty, loading, error, offline/unavailable, permission denied |
| Controls | Hover, focus, active, disabled, validation |
| Responsive | Small phone, tablet, desktop, long text/overflow |

When a row is not applicable, mark **N/A with reason**. Unmocked secondary surfaces must still use the same tokens, typography, voice, assets and interaction patterns.

### 4. Implement deliberately

- Build from approved components/tokens. Extend only as necessary; do not replace a mature component library merely to look original.
- Prefer relevant, properly licensed assets over autogenerated abstract blobs. Authentic domain imagery and art direction often carry more identity than novelty CSS.
- Do not reflexively add the default AI-app combination: purple gradients, glowing orbs, all-rounded cards, uniform shadow panels, excessive feature-grid cards, emoji icons, animated counters, or meaningless badges.
- These elements are **not forbidden**. Use them when the project identity and task justify them. The failure is unexamined defaults, not particular colors or shapes.
- Design for real content length, small screens, keyboard users, and accessibility from the start.
- Motion must communicate feedback, causality, hierarchy or delight; respect prefers-reduced-motion and avoid delaying tasks.

### 5. Editorial copy pass (required)

For every visible string:

- Can a user understand what it is, who it serves and what happens next?
- Replace unsupported superlatives and vague claims with specific mechanisms, evidence, examples or plain language.
- Flag habitual LLM cadences: paired three-word fragments (“Good X. Great Y.”), gratuitous all-caps eyebrow headings, “Unleash / Elevate / Supercharge”, faux confidence, and repetitive “not just X, but Y”.
- Prefer domain vocabulary over SaaS boilerplate; copy should sound like the project, not the generator.
- Ensure error, empty, confirmation and settings messages follow the same voice.
- Do not fabricate testimonials, metrics, customer claims, quotes or evidence.

Copy review is editorial, not a blind regex ban. Short punchy headlines may be exactly right when a brand deliberately owns that style.

### 6. Visual QA loop — no screenshot, no visual signoff

1. Run the actual app/site (preview, isolated from production).
2. Capture screenshots at **390 × 844, 768 × 1024, and 1440 × 900** or documented project-appropriate equivalents; capture at least the primary flow, a secondary route, an empty/error state and a modal/settings state when relevant.
3. Compare the rendered result with the **approved** reference and the visual contract. Review *screenshots, not only source code*.
4. Log actionable defects: route/state, viewport, screenshot reference, severity, expected vs actual, owner. Group repeated root causes instead of filing dozens of duplicates.
5. Fix the highest-impact mismatch; recapture screenshots. Repeat until acceptance checks pass or a blocker is filed.
6. Run accessibility/usability checks, core interaction tests, console/network error inspection, and performance checks. Manual screenshot review does not replace automated tests.
7. For experience-heavy interactive work, test actual touch/keyboard behavior and a modest-performance device, not only the developer laptop.

Do not assert screenshots were examined or behavior was tested unless real evidence exists. If browsing/rendering is unavailable, mark visual QA **BLOCKED**, deliver artifacts and exact steps to verify, and do not call the UI visually approved.

### 7. Quality rubric and hard gates

Use a diagnostic score, not an objective aesthetic truth:

| Dimension | Weight | What good looks like |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Identity / art direction | 20 | Recognizable, coherent, reference-led, not generic by accident |
| Hierarchy / task clarity | 20 | Clear primary action, content prioritization, legible scanning |
| Whole-system consistency | 20 | Secondary screens/states share same decisions and voice |
| Interaction / states | 15 | Controls work, feedback is informative, no broken flows |
| Copy / editorial quality | 15 | Precise, credible, appropriate to brand |
| Access / performance | 10 | Responsive, keyboard/focus/contrast/motion supported, reasonable load |

**Provisional review bar:** ≥85/100, with no zero/critical failure in any dimension. Do not game the score; report disagreements and context. A lower score can be accepted for a consciously rough prototype but cannot be mislabeled production-quality.

**Non-negotiable failures:** dead CTA, broken mobile navigation, clipping/overlap, inaccessible core flow, false copy claims, missing required state, plagiarism/licensing risk, or unverified release-critical functionality.

### 8. Handoff / definition of done

Every substantial visual PR or delivery includes:
- Approved reference(s) and a concise visual contract (or links to canonical versions).
- Screens and states actually covered, with explicit N/As.
- Screenshot receipts with route, viewport and state, plus preview URL if available.
- Copy QA outcomes; performance/accessibility checks run and results.
- Before/after changes, remaining design debt and ownership of blockers.
- No claim of visual completion until the rendered product was actually inspected.
- Changes tracked in one PR/issue (avoid parallel overlapping redesigns).

Agent handoff directive:
> Implement the approved direction, not your default visual taste. Read the project's canonical design, cite the exact reference traits you are adopting, and preserve locked choices. Cover main, secondary and failure states. Render and inspect actual screenshots at mobile/tablet/desktop sizes, correct visible defects, check copy and accessibility, and attach receipts. If you cannot visually verify, mark that gate blocked. Do not change the editorial angle or production systems while polishing unrelated UI.

## Cross-project examples (illustrative, not blanket directives)

- **Portfolio**: an expressive interaction may create a memorable artifact, but core resume, contact and project links must also have a clear accessible path.
- **Organism Atlas / plant tools**: authentic biological imagery and clear taxonomy beat generic SaaS icon grids; complex records need purposeful table/list/empty/error states.
- **Lash website**: genuine photography, precise service language, trust and mobile booking flow are more valuable than decorative glow.
- **6925 Relay**: preserve the approved broadcast package and editorial angle; improve typography, contrast, timing, legibility and video exports rather than reinventing the visual identity.
- **Internal dashboards**: clarity of status, evidence, filters and actions outranks aesthetic novelty.

## Relationship to existing skills

- product-team/ui-design-system: token generation, components, responsive specs.
- product-team/ux-researcher-designer: user journeys and evidence-backed usability.
- subtle-polish-mechanics: interaction motion after the visual direction is set.
- web-quality/qa-testing and performance-optimization: functional and technical test gates.
- This skill: **art direction, non-default design, all-state coherence, copy, screenshot review and evidence-based signoff**.
